# Project Charter

## Product name

**Streets of Türkiye — Little Explorers, Big Türkiye**

## Product vision

A child-friendly digital discovery journey in which users explore compact, stylized-realistic 3D streets representing all 81 provinces of Türkiye, interact with cultural and natural landmarks, collect meaningful artifacts and complete short knowledge checks.

## Current starting point

The supplied legacy HTML already contains:

- 81 provinces;
- 249 discovery stops;
- 84 quiz questions;
- 53 legacy scene-art categories;
- seven regional palettes;
- Nasreddin Hoca and Keloğlan guides;
- a map, sequential unlocks, collection flow and certificate.

Its limitation is not content volume. Its limitation is that the content is embedded in a single HTML file and presented through a passive SVG walk/read/collect loop.

## Transformation objective

Convert the existing prototype into:

- a maintainable application;
- a reusable 3D city engine;
- a curated interaction-first learning loop;
- a scalable 81-city data model;
- a web experience capable of running on desktop and mobile.

## Target audience

Primary: children approximately 7–12 and their families.

Secondary: schools, Turkish cultural programs, museums, consulates and international audiences discovering Türkiye.

## Product principles

1. **Explore, do, learn, collect.** Reading alone is not the activity.
2. **Recognizable rather than enormous.** A compact scene may represent a city well without recreating it at full scale.
3. **Stylized realism.** Culturally recognizable, materially convincing and child-friendly; not photorealistic.
4. **One engine, many cities.** Content variation must not create 81 separate codebases.
5. **Performance is a feature.** Visual ambition must survive real mobile hardware.
6. **Cultural accuracy before decorative spectacle.** Unverified details are omitted or flagged.

## First milestone

A 15–20 minute vertical slice containing:

- İstanbul: urban/history/waterfront;
- Nevşehir: terrain/nature/craft;
- Gaziantep: bazaar/food/craft.

The vertical slice must prove the engine, visual language, interaction system and performance budget before nationwide production begins.
